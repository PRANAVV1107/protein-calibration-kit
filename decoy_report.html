Specificity screening · Method report

Unpublished Negatives

De novo binder design publishes its hits. The designs that bound the wrong thing, or bound nothing, mostly go unrecorded — so there is no reference distribution against which any score can be called good. This is an attempt to build one deliberately.

The problem

A threshold with nothing behind it

A candidate scores ipSAE 0.717. Is that good? The field's working cutoff is 0.61, drawn from a meta-analysis of binders with different targets, different chain lengths and different design methods. None of those are ours.

Answering the question properly needs the other half of the data: what these metrics look like on pairs that do not bind. That half is largely absent from the literature, because a non-binder is not a result anyone publishes.

The asymmetry is visible in how the field measures itself. Establishing what predicts experimental success required a 2025 meta-analysis pooling 3,766 characterised binders scattered across separate papers. There is no equivalent collection of designs that failed — and failure is the more common outcome.

Routinely published Hits

Designs that bound. Reported with structures, affinities, and the score that selected them.

Rarely published Everything else

Designs that bound the wrong target, bound nothing, or scored well and failed. The distribution a threshold would need.

An off-target ipTM of 0.32 reads as meaningful residual binding. The same pair at ipSAE 0.052 is no interface at all. ipTM is computed across whole chains, so unequal chain lengths shift it — the artifact this project had already hit independently, where a true receptor–ligand complex scored lower than an unrelated pair. Most of the promiscuity we were about to design against was not there.

The ranking also reorders: binder_34__s6 leads on ipTM margin, binder_47__s1 on ipSAE margin. Selecting on the wrong metric picks the wrong candidate.

What survives is the spread. Margins run 0.15 to 0.67 across designs whose on-target scores sit within 0.05 of each other. Specificity is a real, separate axis — invisible to the score everyone optimises.

Design

The decoys that matter are relatives

The panel above used what was on disk — a viral receptor-binding domain and an immune checkpoint protein. Both unrelated folds, so both test the easy case.

IL-7Rα is a type-I cytokine receptor built from fibronectin-III domains. A binder that also hits the common gamma chain — IL-7Rα's own signalling partner — is a specificity failure with clinical consequence. One that hits a coronavirus spike domain is a curiosity. Testing only unrelated folds measures the failure mode least likely to occur.

Tier 1 · hard

Structural homologs

Same family, same fold, overlapping architecture. Where real cross-reactivity would show up. Near-identical chain lengths to the target, so ipTM stays interpretable here.

gammaC 191 aa · IL4Ra 188 aa · IL21R 209 aa
Tier 2 · abundance

What a therapeutic actually meets

Not homologs, but present at high concentration in serum. Standard nonspecific-binding indicators. Albumin at 578 aa is a 3:1 length ratio against the 193 aa target — the regime where ipTM misbehaves, so these must be scored on ipSAE.

albumin 578 aa · IgG Fc 207 aa
Tier 3 · control

Unrelated folds

The easy case, retained as a floor. A design that fails here has a serious problem.

PD-L1 115 aa · SARS-CoV-2 RBD 194 aa

Every structure was verified by reading its own TITLE and COMPND records before use. This project previously ran a full campaign against PDB 1ILR believing it to be IL-7Rα; it is interleukin-1 receptor antagonist, an unrelated protein, and nothing downstream caught it.

In progress

Building the null distribution

Two panels are running. Both label pairs as non-binding by construction — unrelated proteins do not bind by default — rather than by deriving labels from the metric being calibrated, which is circular.

PanelPairingsConstructionPurpose
Cross-target null420 every binder × the two targets it was not designed for broad empirical null per metric
Family panel75 15 candidates × 5 tiered decoys hard-case specificity per candidate

Cost is kept down by paying for each decoy's alignment once and reusing it across every pairing. Characterising a null needs volume, not precision.

Stated limitation. These are predicted non-binders. No binding assay has been run on anything in this project. The prior that unrelated proteins do not bind is strong but not certainty, and genuine cross-reactivity would sit in the panel mislabelled — which is itself worth discovering.

Why bother

What a null buys

A percentile instead of a threshold. "ipSAE 0.717" becomes "above 99% of several hundred non-binding pairs from this same pipeline, these same chain lengths, these same design methods." That is a claim the data supports; comparison against a borrowed cutoff is not.

A way to compare metrics honestly. With positives and negatives in hand you can ask which of ipTM, ipSAE or pDockQ2 separates them most sharply — rather than adopting whichever the last paper recommended.

Non-circular calibration labels. A curve fit on labels derived from the score it is calibrating tells you nothing. Negatives by construction break that loop.

And it is cheap. The expensive input — the target's alignment — is identical across every pairing against that target, so it is computed once and reused. Generating negatives costs a fraction of generating the positives they contextualise, which may be part of why so few exist.
